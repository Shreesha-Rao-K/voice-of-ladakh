The Jantar Mantar
Epicenter

On May 3, 2026, over 2.27 million aspirants sat for the NEET-UG examination. Widespread allegations of question-paper leaks and irregular evaluation prompted its cancellation on May 12, a subsequent re-test, and a handover to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI). Initiated online on June 6 by student organizers of the satirical Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) under Abhijeet Dipke, the youth mobilization shifted to Jantar Mantar. On June 28, veteran Ladakhi education reformer Sonam Wangchuk joined the students to begin an indefinite hunger strike demanding institutional transparency. [1] [5]

Educational Background

From SECMOL to
National Outrage

Why did a Himalayan ecological innovator travel to New Delhi to fast alongside protesting students? Because Sonam Wangchuk's public life began as an educator challenging centralized, unadapted testing systems.

SECMOL & The 1988 Reforms

According to Wangchuk's personal accounts and SECMOL educational records, over 95% of Ladakhi students regularly failed matriculation in the late 1980s under state curricula taught in unfamiliar languages. In 1988, he co-founded the Students' Educational and Cultural Movement of Ladakh (SECMOL) to demonstrate practical, indigenous pedagogy. [2]

The 2026 Examination Crisis

When the 2026 medical entrance test was disrupted by question-paper breaches across multiple states, student groups organized demonstrations. Wangchuk joined the Jantar Mantar assembly to amplify calls for systemic examination safeguards and an end to recurring administrative failures. [1]

Parliamentary Impasse

The Deliberation &
The Dispute

As the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) youth assemblies and Wangchuk held their vigil at Jantar Mantar, opposition lawmakers repeatedly stalled parliamentary proceedings over the testing crisis. Student representatives rejected informal talks at ministers' private offices, insisting on dialogue at a neutral public venue—offering either the protest site itself or the Constitution Club of India. [3]

While the government offered administrative committees, protesters insisted on public accountability and structural protections before clearing the grounds.

Core Agitation Demands

  • Executive Accountability: Resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan over supervisory lapses.
  • CBI Oversight & Fast-Tracking: Comprehensive investigation into leak networks and specialized courts for testing integrity.
  • Student Protections: Withdrawal of police FIRs lodged against student demonstrators during nationwide protests.
Timeline of Escalation

Tear Gas & Barricades

From police interventions at Jantar Mantar to the student march toward Parliament, July 2026 witnessed intense confrontation and high-level political negotiations.

JULY 18, 2026 (DAY 21)

Hospital Transfer & Site Eviction

On the 21st day of the indefinite hunger strike, Delhi Police moved into Jantar Mantar early in the morning, shifting a weakened Sonam Wangchuk to Safdarjung Hospital under Section 163 prohibitory orders. Police stated the action complied with Delhi High Court medical advisories and emergency protocols; organizers and student leaders characterized the operation as a forced clearance of the protest camp. [1] [4]

JULY 20, 2026

The "Chalo Sansad" March

Protesting the eviction of Jantar Mantar, thousands of students mobilized for a march toward Parliament. Authorities enforced Section 163 prohibitory orders, using tear gas canisters and conducting lathi charges along barricaded avenues, while water cannons were stationed on standby. Scores of student demonstrators were detained, and mobile internet was temporarily suspended within a 2-kilometer radius. An Additional DCP was later removed from active duty following allegations of excessive physical force. [5]

JULY 21–23, 2026

High Court Shift & Fast-Track Response

On July 21, the Delhi High Court acted on a petition moved by Wangchuk's wife, Gitanjali Angmo, approving his transfer from Safdarjung to Medanta Hospital for specialized care. On July 23, Prime Minister Narendra Modi made his first public intervention regarding the controversy, proposing designated fast-track judicial courts at Rouse Avenue to expedite testing fraud prosecutions. [3] [4]

JULY 23, 2026

26-Day Hunger Strike Concluded

At Medanta Hospital, Sonam Wangchuk concluded his 26-day fast in the presence of Union Ministers J.P. Nadda and Jitendra Singh. The resolution followed extensive multi-party talks, a signed joint appeal from 65 Members of Parliament, and formal written government commitments addressing key student demands. [6]

* Note: The fast conclusion was reported late Thursday, July 23 by The Wire, and early Friday, July 24 by Scroll.

National & Global Reach

A National &
Global Echo

What began as student demonstrations in Delhi resonated across major university centers and prompted extensive domestic and international reporting on testing integrity. [5] [6]

Solidarity assemblies emerged in Patna, Kota, Hyderabad, and Bengaluru, while international news agencies reported on the political fallout of competitive examination security in the world's most populous nation.

"The scale of the youth response transformed a testing breach into an urgent national dialogue on institutional governance and meritocratic trust."

— Journalistic Analysis on Examination Governance, July 2026
Political Resolution

Resignation & Resolution

DOSSIER #01

Ministerial Resignation

On July 25, 2026, Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan submitted his formal resignation to President Droupadi Murmu, who accepted it that afternoon. Parliamentary Affairs Minister Pralhad Joshi was assigned additional charge of the Ministry of Education. [7]

DOSSIER #02

Fasting Resolution

Sonam Wangchuk formally broke his 26-day strike at Medanta Hospital following written commitments regarding testing oversight, fast-track court tribunals, and assurances that peaceful student protesters would not face punitive action. [6]

DOSSIER #03

CBI Judicial Filings

On July 28, the Central Bureau of Investigation submitted its comprehensive chargesheet naming 13 accused individuals, including testing center staff and translators, while the court granted authorization for continued forensic investigation. [8]
