The Jantar Mantar
Epicenter

A nationwide examination crisis and allegations of irregularities fueled a youth-led movement demanding accountability across India's education system. At the center of the agitation was Sonam Wangchuk, undertaking an indefinite hunger strike in the summer heat of Delhi. [1]

SCROLL TO EXPLORE
The Root Cause

From SECMOL to
National Outrage

Why is a climate activist fasting for national education reform? Because Sonam Wangchuk has fought the broken education system his entire life.

A Broken System

In 1988, Wangchuk realized 95% of Ladakhi students failed because the mainstream rote-learning curriculum was alien to them. He co-founded SECMOL to build alternative schools.

The 2026 Crisis

Decades later, the national education system is imploding. The NEET-UG corruption and paper leaks revealed a system that fails its youth at an industrial scale. Wangchuk could no longer stay silent.

Political Gridlock

The Government
Response

As the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) and Wangchuk occupied Jantar Mantar, Parliament entered a state of gridlock. The Prime Minister promised "fast-track courts," but protesters demanded systemic overhaul and the resignation of the Education Minister.

The government refused to meet at the protest site, insisting on closed-door meetings. The protesters refused to leave. The tension hit a boiling point.

The Core Demands

  • Resignation of the Union Education Minister.
  • Accountability for the NEET-UG paper leaks.
  • Protection against punitive legal action for students.
Timeline of Escalation

Tear Gas & Barricades

When student demonstrations mobilized for the "Chalo Sansad" (March to Parliament), authorities enforced Section 163 prohibitory regulations and deployed barricades.

JULY 18, 2026

The Hospital Transfer

Delhi Police moved into the protest site early on July 18 and shifted Wangchuk from Jantar Mantar to hospital after his health deteriorated during the 20th day of his fast. Protest organisers described the removal as forced, while authorities stated the action followed medical advice and court directives. [1][4]

JULY 20, 2026

Chalo Sansad March

Following the police action, thousands of demonstrators attempted to march toward Parliament in defiance of prohibitory orders. Police deployed tear gas shells and water cannons, detaining numerous student demonstrators. [5]

JULY 23, 2026

26-Day Fast Concludes

At Medanta Hospital, Sonam Wangchuk formally concluded his 26-day indefinite hunger strike after receiving assurances regarding student protection and formal examination reforms. [6]

Beyond Borders

A Global Echo

Coverage of the protests spread beyond India, bringing international attention to the dispute over examination integrity and education policy. [5][6]

Solidarity gatherings occurred in multiple educational hubs across the country. Images and reports from Jantar Mantar circulated widely in international press outlets, drawing commentary on systemic testing procedures and transparency.

"This controversy reflects a critical generational conversation on examination integrity and the institutional future of higher education."

— Press Analysis & Commentary, July 2026
The Dust Settles

Resignation & Resolution

DOSSIER #01

Ministerial Resignation

On July 25, 2026, Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan submitted his resignation amid mounting political scrutiny and youth demonstrations. The portfolio was subsequently reassigned to Pralhad Joshi. [7]

DOSSIER #02

Fasting Concluded

Sonam Wangchuk formally broke his 26-day fast on July 23 after receiving medical stabilization. In subsequent statements, he emphasized the importance of honoring commitments made to students. [6]

DOSSIER #03

Official Inquiries

The Central Bureau of Investigation continued charge-sheet filings concerning question-paper leaks, while protest organizers scheduled regional meetings to debate policy proposals. [8]
